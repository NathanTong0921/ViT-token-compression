# ViT-token-compression

**Same Image, Different Evidence: When Does Token Compression Need Task Conditioning?**

This project studies whether changing the competing classes changes which visual tokens are useful for classification, and whether a lightweight task-conditioned selector can learn and generalize that advantage.

The main study uses frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 on Oxford-IIIT Pets, with controlled cross-task selection, strong shared-selection baselines, and matched conditional/unconditional selectors. Flowers102 and targeted ViT-L/14 experiments provide validation.

`Project_Proposal.pdf` is the proposal for submission and the reference plan for project execution. 
**Latest update (2026-10-29):** the stronger image-only baseline and task-descriptor component ablations are complete: 36 new selectors across Pets/Flowers, 49/98 tokens and three fixed seeds. Reallocating a similar parameter budget to a wider image MLP removes the previously established conditioning margin leads on Pets98 and Flowers49; the Flowers98 lead remains, while Pets49 remains worse. All four conditioning-versus-wide accuracy intervals include zero. Component ablations find that adding the difference component to mean-only improves margin at 49 tokens on both datasets, while adding the mean component to difference-only improves margin at 98 on both. Mean-only selection also retains a positive task-exchange effect. See the [combined ablation results](results/selector-ablations-v1/README.md) and [I14–I15](docs/research-insights.md). Next: freeze the reserved-data confirmation protocol, then complete the report and presentation materials.

The base study completed on 2026-10-27 covers the Pets 49/98/147-token comparison, H2 analysis, Flowers102 validation, targeted ViT-L/14 experiments, measured inference latency and numerical-sensitivity checks. **The historical classification comparisons below use the original image-only selector**, which replaces the task descriptor with a fixed training mean. On Pets, each B/16 budget uses full training coverage, the same 3,990 updates, and three fixed paired seeds. Conditioning has a small positive geometric-margin advantage over that selector at 98 tokens, a negative advantage at 49, and an uncertain advantage at 147. No Pets budget establishes an accuracy improvement. These results, including 147, remain unchanged in the [Pets results](results/pets-selector-budgets-v1/README.md), [run record](docs/experiments/2026-10-27-selector-budgets-h2.md), and [fixed protocol](docs/selector-budgets-h2.md).

On Flowers102, both 49 and 98 tokens have positive mean margin advantages over the matched image-only selector. At 98 tokens the accuracy advantage is +0.2778 percentage points, with a two-budget-adjusted interval of [+0.0082, +0.5474]; at 49 tokens the accuracy difference remains uncertain. The direct change in conditioning advantage from 49 to 98 is also uncertain: neither that contrast nor the Pets negative/positive sign pattern is replicated. Budget alone therefore does not describe the observed benefit across these settings. Flowers uses newly trained selectors, 1,020 training images, 3,978 updates, three fixed paired seeds, and 1,020 validation images under 204 unseen combinations; it is not direct transfer of Pets-trained weights. See the [Flowers results](results/flowers-selector-budgets-v1/README.md) and [predeclared targeted protocol](docs/flowers-targeted-validation.md).

On Pets, H2 receives development-set support: after adjusting for both uncompressed task margins, larger class-direction angles predict larger cross-task selection losses at all three budgets. Within-image checks agree; five-fold held-image incremental R-squared is 0.0240 / 0.0114 / 0.0062. This is a limited predictive association, not a causal mechanism or evidence of general superiority over shared selection. The exact geometric identity and normalized-alignment diagnostics are reported separately.

The H2 association replicates on Flowers at both budgets, including within-image checks and held-image prediction (incremental R-squared 0.0212 / 0.0464). Flowers also extends the distinction between teacher fitting and classification: conditioned selectors have worse validation KL despite positive margin gains. The source of these differences remains unresolved; see [I9 in the insight log](docs/research-insights.md#i9flowers-复现了任务几何关联但没有复现-pets-的预算符号变化).

ViT-L/14 retains half the patch tokens (128/256) after block 12/24, with fresh supervision and three paired training seeds on each dataset. H2 remains positive on both datasets after difficulty adjustment, within-image checks and held-image prediction. Flowers has a +0.1471 percentage-point accuracy advantage over the image-only selector (95% interval [+0.0245, +0.2859]); its mean margin advantage is uncertain. Pets has an uncertain accuracy difference and a negative margin difference. Thus, the geometry association generalizes more consistently across the tested settings than the classification gains. See the [cross-model and efficiency summary](results/vitl-and-latency-v1/README.md).

Complete GPU inference measurements include the prefix, online selector, ranking/gather, suffix and class scores. On an RTX A6000, half-token conditioning achieves approximately 1.25× (B/16) and 1.29× (L/14) speedup at batch 32, but no speedup at batch 1. All seven model/dataset/budget settings and all three learned-selector seeds are retained. A full validation-set comparison of cached evaluation with live batch-1/32 execution finds a maximum change of 0.01634 percentage points in the conditioning-versus-image-only accuracy effect; the corresponding ordinary-95%-interval sign/zero patterns remain unchanged. This diagnostic does not replace the original budget-adjusted analyses.

The Pets main evaluation contains 716 development images and 66 unseen class combinations; its exchange analysis uses 616 eligible images and 4,315 task pairs. A privileged common-library search also finds a simultaneously correct shared mask for about 99.9% of image-weighted task pairs. Margin specialization and a necessity for different masks to answer correctly are therefore different claims in this setting. Official test images and test pairs remain unused on both datasets. The next required work is to freeze the final-test protocol, confirm the core findings on reserved images and class combinations, and prepare the report, PPT and video.

The [coverage experiment](results/pets-selector-scale-v1/README.md), [preceding training pilot](results/pets-selector-pilot-v1/README.md), and [fixed-model exchange experiment](results/pets-selector-exchange-v1/README.md) retain their original results and scope. The Pets budget comparison reuses the full-coverage 49-token models unchanged and trains budget-matched 98/147-token models. All seeds and both favorable and unfavorable results are retained.

The 2026-10-28 focused follow-up is complete: Pets finite-library diagnostics and a fixed KL-versus-Top-k-ranking intervention across Pets/Flowers at 49/98 tokens add 24 matched selectors. Under the fixed training recipe, the objective increases the conditioning margin advantage on Pets49 and decreases it on Flowers98; all four adjusted accuracy-interaction intervals include zero. A probe-only specialization gap does not establish extra held-image predictive value. This strengthens the analysis of potential versus realized task-conditioning benefits without establishing a generally better objective. This intervention retains the original image-only architecture in both loss arms; the subsequent wider-baseline comparison is reported separately above. See the [combined results](results/conditioning-objective-v1/README.md), [fixed protocol](docs/conditioning-objective-followup.md), and [execution record](docs/experiments/2026-10-28-conditioning-objective.md). Final reserved-data confirmation remains pending.

## Working approach

- Follow the [project TODO](docs/TODO.md) for remaining experiments and deliverables; update it as evidence changes the next step.
- Keep code direct and focused on the current experiments. Avoid speculative abstractions, redundant checks, and silent fallbacks; retain checks that establish scientific correctness.
- Treat the proposal as the starting research plan. Raise new observations, hypotheses, and better experimental designs as they emerge, and record the evidence and tradeoffs behind changes to the plan.
- After each completed task, explain the result plainly, identify the corresponding proposal stage, distinguish any new insight from the original plan, and assess whether it advances the project goal. Report unexpected problems and useful observations during execution.
- Keep evolving findings in the [research insight log](docs/research-insights.md), with evidence links, their relationship to the proposal, and implications for the next experiment. Update the interpretation when new evidence changes it; retain dated revisions.

## Version management

- Keep `main` as the checked, reproducible baseline. Use short-lived `feat/...`, `fix/...`, or `exp/...` branches for substantial changes and experimental variants.
- Make each commit one coherent change, with a message explaining its purpose. Run relevant checks before committing and push completed milestones to this repository.
- Commit experiment code and configuration before a substantive run. Record the full commit SHA, command, configuration, seed, data split, model checkpoint, and environment alongside the results.
- Save small metrics, figures, split manifests, and experiment summaries in Git. Store datasets, feature caches, checkpoints, and raw run outputs separately, recording their locations and identifiers.
- Tag validated milestones when they exist, so the pilot, main experiments, and final report can be reproduced from known versions.

Use [the experiment record template](docs/experiment-log.md) for each substantive experiment. Negative and inconclusive results should retain the same provenance as positive results.

Paper evidence will be retained on the server, locally, and on GitHub: per-example results, figure source data, figures, configurations, and selected selector checkpoints. Small files belong in Git; larger essential artifacts belong in versioned GitHub Release attachments. See the [storage and backup plan](docs/storage-and-backup.md). Runtime records, the original baseline, pruning pilot and offline-selection diagnostics include locally retained scores, selection positions, statistical tables and plots. Transfer-only archives are removed after verification.

## File organization

As implementation begins, use `src/` for reusable code, `scripts/` for entry points, `configs/` for settings, `data/splits/` for small split manifests, `results/` for curated results, and `docs/` for research records.

Keep bulk artifacts in the ignored directories `data/raw/`, `data/processed/`, `cache/`, `checkpoints/`, `runs/`, or `outputs/`. Sample course reports and downloaded papers remain in the surrounding local project folder.
