# CUB candidate-set pilot

Protocol fixed before examining CUB predictions, 2026-11-08.

## Question

Does task-conditioned selection add useful information on a bird-species dataset, where annotated parts and attributes can support a more concrete account of the selected visual evidence? The preceding Pets/Flowers two-versus-five-way experiment reduced accuracy saturation without increasing the C-over-U advantage. This pilot first measures CUB's classification and compression behavior, then tests the same paired selectors.

## Scope

- Official CUB-200-2011 images and annotations from [CaltechDATA](https://data.caltech.edu/records/65de6-vp158).
- All 200 species; select 20 training and 5 development images per species from the official training split with seed 2615. The official test split remains unused. This yields 4,000 training and 1,000 development images.
- Frozen CLIP ViT-B/16, pruning after block 6, 98 retained spatial tokens; the class token remains present.
- Two and five candidate classes. Reuse the balanced task construction with seed 2611, four training rounds and two development rounds; training and development candidate sets are disjoint.
- Full CLIP and attention provide initial references. C and resource-matched wide U use the existing n-way training recipe, seeds 2041/2042/2043, 3,990 updates, and final checkpoints.
- Reuse the 512-mask, fixed-budget supervision design and candidate geometric-margin target. Candidate-set descriptors are mean plus twice population standard deviation, as in the existing n-way extension.

## Measurements and interpretation

First report full-model versus attention accuracy and margin, including all-species classification as context. Then compare C and U accuracy, raw/geometric margin and own-versus-exchanged task descriptions on development images and unseen candidate combinations. Use the same paired-image uncertainty analysis as the existing n-way study.

CUB's part locations, visibility and attributes are retained for follow-up interpretation; they are not inputs to the selectors in this pilot. Useful follow-up evidence would connect task-dependent selection changes to specific visual regions and classification outcomes. A region-overlap observation alone is not a causal attribution of the model's decision.

The pilot informs which datasets and findings deserve space in the final report. Existing Pets/Flowers evidence is retained. It does not replace their completed reserved-test evaluation or establish a CUB test-set result.

## Execution and storage

Run feature extraction and initial full/attention references before mask probing. Reuse each image's prefix and mask responses for both task sizes. Keep datasets, model weights and feature caches on the server; push code, manifests, curated analysis and figures to GitHub, with complete experiment bundles in Releases. Remove transfer-only download archives after extraction. No persistent local model or raw-array archive is needed.

## Results — completed 2026-11-08

All twelve planned selectors completed training. The table reports development accuracy averaged equally across tasks and candidate classes, then across seeds. C−U intervals adjust over the two task sizes.

| Candidates | Full CLIP (%) | Attention (%) | C (%) | U (%) | C−U (pp) [adjusted interval] |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 98.675 | 98.350 | 98.375 | 98.517 | −0.142 [−0.308, +0.017] |
| 5 | 95.800 | 95.170 | 95.330 | 95.590 | −0.260 [−0.507, −0.040] |

C−U raw and geometric margins are negative at both task sizes, with adjusted intervals below zero. Geometric-margin differences are −0.001204 for two candidates and −0.000857 for five. Correct task descriptions improve raw and geometric margins relative to exchanged descriptions, but their accuracy effects remain unresolved. The five-minus-two interaction in C−U accuracy is −0.118 pp, with interval [−0.327, +0.092].

Full CLIP's 200-species development accuracy is 54.5%, while the fixed candidate subsets produce the much higher accuracies above. Changing to a fine-grained dataset did not by itself create an advantage for the conditioned selector. CUB supports the distinction between task dependence and gains over strong shared selection; this pilot does not establish a new method improvement or a bird-part explanation. No part-level analysis, official-test evaluation, PR comparison or L/14 expansion was performed. These results do not motivate replacing Pets on performance grounds.

## Recorded cost and storage

The cache adds 5,000 images and 512 masks per image, for 2,560,000 suffix examples. Full-token inference matched native CLIP exactly on all cached images. Feature extraction and initial references took 46.8 seconds of recorded stage wall time. Probe generation took 748.0 seconds summed across the parallel train/development jobs, including 626.9 seconds in their inference loops. Candidate-target preparation took 111.1 seconds, the training stage 455.9 seconds, and evaluation 44.7 seconds. Summed optimizer-update time across the twelve selectors was 347.3 seconds.

The sum of recorded stage wall times is 1,406.6 seconds (0.391 hours). It includes CPU processing and I/O, excludes dataset download and cache-stage model loading, and is not an exclusive-GPU latency measurement. The [cache records](../../results/cub-pilot-v1/execution/cache/) and [pipeline status](../../results/cub-pilot-v1/execution/study/status.json) preserve the measurements.

Curated results and execution records are retained locally and in Git. Complete raw outputs remain on the server under `runs/cub-nway-v1/`; remote archive packaging and verification are pending. The current storage policy keeps complete archives remotely rather than maintaining local copies.
